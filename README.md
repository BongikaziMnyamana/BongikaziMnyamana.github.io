# CV Coded Using Markdown – Reflection

**Situation:** As part of my PRP370 module, I was required to create a professional CV using Markdown language. This was my first time writing in Markdown, and I had no prior experience with its syntax or formatting conventions.

**Task:** My task was to produce a well-structured, visually clear CV that showcased my education, technical skills and projects, all coded in Markdown rather than a traditional word processor.

**Action:** I researched Markdown syntax, learning how to use headings (`#`), bold text (`**`), bullet points (`-`), horizontal rules (`---`) and inline formatting for links and icons. I structured the CV with clear sections and used emojis as visual anchors to make each section easy to identify. I also linked my GitHub and LinkedIn profiles directly in the header.

**Result:** I successfully produced a complete, professional CV in Markdown. I gained a new technical skill that is widely used in the software development industry, particularly for README files and documentation on GitHub. The experience improved my attention to formatting detail and gave me confidence in writing structured content using lightweight markup languages.

# Semester 2- Entrepreneurial WIL Project: GugsLocalConnect

## Project Context

GugsLocalConnect is a community platform that connects residents of Gugulethu with trusted local businesses and service providers. The aim is to make it easier for residents to find local services and help businesses improve their visibility in the community.

Our team consisted of six members who worked in Agile sprints and managed project tasks using Trello.

**My role was Agile Lead**, responsible for facilitating Agile meetings and encouraging teamwork and communication.

# User Stories and Tasks

## Evidence

The following screenshot shows our user stories and tasks organised on Trello.

![User Stories and Tasks on Trello](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BongikaziMnyamana/BongikaziMnyamana.github.io/main/User%20story.jpeg)

[View User Stories and Tasks on Trello](https://trello.com/b/HIAaT2dR/gugslocalconnect-task-board)

Our team used Trello to record our user stories and turn each one into smaller tasks. Each task had a checklist and was assigned to a team member, so we knew who was responsible and what still needed to be done. This helped us keep our work focused on what customers and business owners needed from GugsLocalConnect.

Our two main user stories were:

1. **Customer:** As a customer, I want to browse local businesses and contact business owners, so that I can find trusted services in my community.
   - Related tasks: search functionality, category filtering and contact business feature.

2. **Business Owner:** As a business owner, I want to manage my business profile and services, so that customers can find my business online.
   - Related tasks: business profile management and service listing feature.

## Reflection

**Situation:** Our team needed to understand what users needed from the platform and divide the work in a way everyone could follow.

**Task:** As Agile Lead, I wanted to make sure the work was clear enough that every team member knew what they were responsible for.

**Action:** We described features as user stories and split each one into smaller tasks with checklists on Trello, assigned to team members.

**Result:** The checklists made progress easy to see, and most cards showed 6/6 or 4/4 once complete.

**Learning and Next Time:** I learned that a story is only useful if its tasks are small and specific. Next time I would write acceptance criteria for each story so that everyone agrees what "done" means.

# Sprint Backlog

## Evidence

The following screenshot shows our team's sprint backlog and how tasks were organised into different stages.

![Sprint Backlog](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BongikaziMnyamana/BongikaziMnyamana.github.io/main/Sprint-Backlog.png)

[View Sprint Backlog on Trello](https://trello.com/b/HIAaT2dR/gugslocalconnect-task-board)

Our team used sprints on Trello to split the project into stages. Each card had a checklist and team members assigned to it, which allowed us to follow progress and see who was responsible. The board also showed where work was behind, for example two Sprint 3 requirements cards that had no completed tasks yet.

The sprints shown in our evidence were:

### Sprint 1 – Research and Problem Identification

- Collect survey responses.
- Identify community stakeholders.
- Define the community problem.

### Sprint 2 – Business Planning

- Define project objectives.
- Develop the value proposition.
- Research competitors.
- Complete a SWOT analysis.
- Develop the business model.

### Sprint 3 – Requirements Analysis

- Gather functional and non-functional requirements.
- Identify system users.
- Define business owner and customer requirements.

### Sprint 4 – System Design

- Create the class diagram.
- Create the Entity Relationship Diagram (ERD).
- Develop the system flow diagram.
- Design the database schema.
- Identify API requirements.

### Sprint 5 – UI/UX Design

- Create the Figma project.
- Design the landing page.
- Design login and registration pages.

### Sprint 6 – Frontend Development

- Set up the Angular project.
- Configure navigation and routing.
- Develop the landing page.
- Implement registration functionality.

### Sprint 8 – Core Features

- Business profile management.
- Service listing.
- Search functionality.
- Category filtering.
- Contact business feature.

### Sprint 9 – Testing and Deployment

- Unit testing.
- Integration testing.
- Further testing and deployment tasks.

## Reflection

**Situation:** Our team needed to break a large project into stages we could plan and finish one at a time.

**Task:** As Agile Lead, I needed to help the team plan each sprint and keep the board accurate so everyone could see progress.

**Action:** We grouped tasks by sprint, assigned them to team members and tracked checklist progress on Trello.

**Result:** Most cards were completed, but the board also showed work that was not finished, for example the Sprint 3 cards "Define business owner requirements" (0/1) and "Define customer requirements" (0/7).

**Learning and Next Time:** I learned that a sprint plan needs regular checking, because tasks can take longer than expected or depend on other work. Next time I would check the board before each stand-up and move late cards into the next sprint early.
