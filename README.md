## CV Coded Using Markdown Language Reflection

**Situation:**
As part of my PRP370 module, I was required to create a professional CV using 
Markdown language. This was my first time writing in Markdown, and I had no 
prior experience with its syntax or formatting conventions.

**Task:**
My task was to produce a well-structured, visually clear CV that showcased my 
education, technical skills, and projects all coded in Markdown 
rather than a traditional word processor.

**Action:**
I researched Markdown syntax, learning how to use headings (`#`), bold text 
(`**`), bullet points (`-`), horizontal rules (`---`), and inline formatting 
for links and icons. I structured the CV with clear sections and used emojis 
as visual anchors to make each section easy to identify. I also linked my 
GitHub and LinkedIn profiles directly in the header.

**Result:**
I successfully produced a complete, professional CV in Markdown. I gained a 
new technical skill that is widely used in the software development industry — 
particularly for README files and documentation on GitHub. The experience 
improved my attention to formatting detail and gave me confidence in writing 
structured content using lightweight markup languages.

## Agile Team Charter

### Evidence
[View my Agile Team Charter)](https://github.com/BongikaziMnyamana/BongikaziMnyamana.github.io/blob/main/PRP3_TEAM_CHARTER..docx)

The charter sets out the purpose of GugsLocalConnect, the six team roles, our core values (collaboration, respect, transparency, accountability and continuous improvement), a communication plan (daily stand-ups, a weekly sprint review and a sprint retrospective), a SMART goal and a 14-week milestone plan.

### Reflection
**Situation:** Our team of six was starting the GugsLocalConnect project and needed a shared agreement on how we would work together, who was responsible for what, and how we would communicate.

**Task:** As Agile Lead, my task was to help the team follow the agreements in the charter, especially the meeting routine (daily stand-ups, weekly reviews and retrospectives) and keeping the Trello board updated at the end of each working day.

**Action:** I helped organise and facilitate our team meetings on Microsoft Teams and used the WhatsApp group for quick updates and reminders.

**Result:** The charter gave the team a common direction and clear expectations for roles and communication.

**Learning and next time:** I learned that agreements only work if someone checks that they are being followed. If I wrote the charter again, I would make the agreements easier to keep, for example by agreeing stand-up times that fit around everyone's timetables.

<a id="user-stories-and-tasks"></a>
## User Stories and Tasks

### Evidence
![User stories and tasks on Trello](images/user-stories.png)

[View our Trello board](https://trello.com/b/HIAaT2dR/gugslocalconnect-task-board)

Our team used Trello to record our user stories and turn each one into smaller tasks. Each task had a checklist and was given to a team member, so we knew who was responsible and what still needed to be done. This helped us keep our work focused on what customers and business owners needed from GugsLocalConnect. Our two main user stories were:

1. As a **customer**, I want to browse local businesses and contact business owners, so that I can find trusted services in my community.
   - Related tasks: search functionality, category filtering, contact business feature
2. As a **business owner**, I want to manage my business profile and services, so that customers can find my business online.
   - Related tasks: business profile management, service listing feature

### Reflection
**Situation:** Our team needed to understand what users needed from the platform and divide the work in a way everyone could follow.

**Task:** As Agile Lead, I wanted to make sure the work was clear enough that every team member knew what they were responsible for.

**Action:** We described features as user stories and split each one into smaller tasks with checklists on Trello, assigned to team members.

**Result:** The checklists made progress easy to see, and most cards showed 6/6 or 4/4 once complete.

**Learning and next time:** I learned that a story is only useful if its tasks are small and specific. Next time I would write acceptance criteria for each story so that everyone agrees what "done" means.

<a id="sprint-backlog"></a>
## Sprint Backlog

### Evidence
![Sprint backlog on Trello](images/sprint-backlog.png)

Our team used sprints on Trello to split the project into stages, such as Business Planning, Requirements Analysis, System Design and UI/UX Design. Each card had a checklist and the team members assigned to it, which let us follow progress and see who was responsible. The board also showed where work was behind, for example two Sprint 3 requirements cards that had no completed tasks yet. The sprints shown in the evidence are:

- **Sprint 1 – Research and Problem Identification:** collect survey responses, identify community stakeholders, define the community problem
- **Sprint 2 – Business Planning:** define project objectives, value proposition, research competitors, SWOT analysis, business model
- **Sprint 3 – Requirements Analysis:** gather functional and non-functional requirements, identify system users
- **Sprint 4 – System Design:** class diagram, ERD, system flow diagram, database schema, API requirements
- **Sprint 5 – UI/UX Design:** Figma project, landing page, login and registration pages
- **Sprint 6 – Frontend Development:** set up Angular, navigation, landing page, registration
- **Sprint 8 – Core Features:** business profile management, service listing, search, category filtering, contact business
- **Sprint 9 – Testing and Deployment:** unit testing, integration testing

### Reflection
**Situation:** Our team needed to break a large project into stages we could plan and finish one at a time.

**Task:** As Agile Lead, I needed to help the team plan each sprint and keep the board accurate so everyone could see progress.

**Action:** We grouped tasks by sprint, assigned them to team members and tracked checklist progress on Trello.

**Result:** Most cards were completed, but the board also showed work that was not finished, for example the Sprint 3 cards "Define business owner requirements" (0/1) and "Define customer requirements" (0/7).

**Learning and next time:** I learned that a sprint plan needs regular checking, because tasks can take longer than expected or depend on other work. Next time I would check the board before each stand-up and move late cards into the next sprint early.
